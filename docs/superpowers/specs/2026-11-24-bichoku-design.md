# Bichoku (Godot): design

Date: 2026-11-24

## Intent

Bichoku is a 9×9 sudoku where the nine symbols are Argentine animals instead of digits ("bicho" is Argentine for critter). A web version exists in `~/src/sudoku` (its design: `~/src/sudoku/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-bichoku-design.md`). This repo is its port to Godot, the first Churrinche Games release.

**Why now:** Bichoku is #1 in the portfolio's catalog because it proves the whole pipeline: the kit, the engraving art style, a Google Play release and an itch.io release. The portfolio design (`~/src/game-kit/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-21-portfolio-design.md`) governs everything not said here.

**Scope (Juan, 2026-11-24): the web game as it is**, plus only what Google Play and the portfolio require. Items from the web version's "next" list (daily puzzle, technique-based difficulty, kids mode, offline service worker) come in later updates.

## Goals

- Every rule and setting of the web game, behaving the same (see Game).
- Spanish (rioplatense, voseo) and English.
- The engraving art style for all nine animals, at both levels of detail.
- The kit's shared screens and services exist and are used: About, feedback, support, sound, language, saving.
- Builds for Google Play (AAB) and itch.io (web, plus APK), passing the portfolio's release process.

## Non-goals (first release)

- The web version's gallery of other board shapes (jigsaw, samurai, X, windoku, other sizes), and the engine code that builds them.
- Daily puzzle, technique-based difficulty, kids mode, rating prompt, More games screen (Bichoku is the only game), Play tip jar.
- Accounts, ads, analytics, network access of any kind.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it works)

- A board you have to squint at: animals that can't be told apart at board size.
- A wait before a new game starts.
- Any donation link or donation wording in the Play build.
- Losing a player's game in progress or best times.

## Constraints

- Godot 4.7, GDScript with static typing, TDD (see CLAUDE.md).
- The kit is vendored from a tag; kit modules are written in `~/src/game-kit`, tagged, and synced here. They must not know about Bichoku.
- Offline only: no permissions beyond what Godot needs; the Data safety form says no data collected; audience 13+.
- Art and sound are generated by committed scripts (portfolio Art direction and Sound sections).
- Android package `com.churrinche.bichoku` (permanent after the first Play upload).

## Approaches considered (puzzles)

| Approach | Trade-off | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Port the engine to GDScript and generate on the device** | A faithful port; offline; always a fresh puzzle. GDScript is much slower than the browser (the web takes under 15 ms for a 9×9), so generation runs in the background and the next puzzle is prepared while you play. | **Chosen** (Juan, 2026-11-24). The daily puzzle and the technique grader will need the engine anyway. |
| 2. Ship a fixed bank of puzzles made in advance | Least game code; the bank only grows with updates; needs a generation script in the repo. | Rejected |
| 3. Both: engine plus a starter bank | Most work for a small gain. | Rejected |

## Game

Same rules as the web version (port `~/src/sudoku/src/features/game/game.ts`):

- Pure state: `update(state, action)` with select, input, erase, undo, hint, toggle notes, tick. Givens never change; nothing changes after a win.
- **Notes:** in notes mode, tapping animals toggles marks in the cell. Placing an animal clears that cell's notes and removes the animal from the notes of every cell in its row, column and box.
- **Clashes:** a cell whose animal repeats in its row, column or box gets a faint red fill and a red ring (Juan, checkpoint 2; the ring keeps it readable without color). Never compares against the solution, and there is no "wrong against the solution" mode in the first release (Juan). Can be turned off.
- **Hint:** fills the selected cell if it is empty or wrong; otherwise fixes a wrong cell, or else fills the empty cell with the fewest options. Limited per game: Easy 3, Medium 2, Hard 1.
- **Difficulty** by clue count: Easy stops removing clues at 40, Medium at 32, Hard removes as many as it can (about 24).
- **Best time** per difficulty, only for games solved without hints; the win screen says so when hints were used.
- **Win screen:** time, best time, and a fact about one animal (a different one each game), with that animal's full drawing.
- **Settings:** animals left (on), mark clashes (on), show numbers (off), language, music and effects (separate on/off toggles, in the settings menu). With "animals left" off, finished animals stay lit in the palette.
- **Language (Juan, 2026-11-24):** on first launch it follows the device: Spanish if the device language is Spanish, otherwise English (my call for languages other than Spanish and English). Choosing a language in settings overrides that and is saved.
- **Saving** with the kit's `SaveStore`: the game in progress (on every change), best times and settings. A broken save never crashes the game; it starts fresh.
- **Input:** tap a cell, then an animal. On the web build, the web version's keys too: 1–9, arrows, Backspace, N (notes), U or Ctrl+Z (undo), H (hint).

## Engine

Port of `~/src/sudoku/src/engine` limited to what 9×9 needs, keeping its generic model (a shape is cells plus houses; the solver doesn't know about squares):

- `mulberry32` and `shuffle`, the bitmask backtracking solver (fewest-candidates cell or fewest-places digit), `uniqueness` with a step budget, and `generate(shape, seed, min_clues, check_nodes)` exactly as the engine design describes (many short fills of 20k steps, up to 100; remove clues in shuffled order; an `unknown` keeps its clue; default `check_nodes` 5000).
- **Same seed, same puzzle as the web version.** Fixtures produced by the web engine (a handful of seeds per difficulty: puzzle and solution) are committed and the GDScript port must reproduce them exactly. The JS integer arithmetic of `mulberry32` (`Math.imul`, `>>>`) must be emulated with 32-bit masking.
- Generation runs off the main thread (`WorkerThreadPool` or `Thread`). The next puzzle for the current difficulty is prepared while you play, so "New game" is instant; the very first launch may show a short "preparing" state.

## Screen

One portrait column (viewport 720×1280, stretch `canvas_items`, aspect `expand`), inside the safe area:

```
 Bichoku                         (?) (⚙)
 [ Fácil ] [ Medio ] [ Difícil ]
 ⏱ 04:12                     Pistas: 3
 ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
 │            9×9 board              │
 └───────────────────────────────────┘
 [Y][L][P][C][H][Yc][F][G][B]   counts left under each
 [↶ Deshacer] [⌫ Borrar] [✎ Notas] [💡 Pista]
```

- **Board:** cream paper, thin ink lines, thicker lines around the 3×3 boxes. Each animal in its own ink (simple level), all at the same size. Givens sit on a slightly darker paper cell `#e3d2ba` (Juan, checkpoint 2).
- **Highlights:** the selected cell gets a soft fill and its row, column and box a lighter one; cells with the same animal as the selection get an underline (my call); clashes a faint red fill and a red ring.
- **Notes:** a dot in the animal's ink at the animal's fixed spot of a 3×3 mini grid (1 top left … 9 bottom right). With "show numbers" on, the dots become small digits.
- **Help (?)** explains the rules and the controls; the keyboard keys only where there is a keyboard (desktop, and the web build on a computer; not phones or phone browsers — Juan, 2026-11-25). **Settings (⚙)** holds the settings above plus entries for About, Feedback and (itch build only) Support.
- Android back button closes an open dialog, else asks before leaving. The game pauses (clock stops) when the app goes to the background.

## Screen sizes

- Base canvas 720×1280 portrait, stretch `canvas_items`, aspect `expand`: everything scales with the screen and there are never black bars; extra space goes to the longer side. Android is locked to portrait.
- **The board is square and as large as fits:** its side is the smaller of the column width and the height left after the header, palette and tools.
- **Tall phones** (19.5:9, 20:9): the extra height is shared above and below the board; nothing stretches.
- **Wide or short screens** (16:9 phones, tablets, open foldables, the web build in a landscape browser window): the column is at most 720 units wide and centred.
- **Safe area:** content stays inside the device's safe area (notches, camera holes, gesture bar), via the kit's mobile module.
- **Touch:** a cell is about 38 dp on a 360 dp phone (same as the web version); palette and tool buttons are at least 48 dp.
- **Check:** a test lays the screen out at 720×1280, 1080×2400, a 4:3 tablet and a 16:9 landscape window and checks that nothing overlaps or leaves the visible area. Screenshots at those sizes are part of the release candidate.

## Kit modules built along the way

Written in `~/src/game-kit` (generic, tested there), tagged, synced here. Only what Bichoku uses:

- **About:** game name and version, "Source code on GitHub" link, licenses and credits (including `THIRD_PARTY.md` items).
- **Feedback:** opens an email with the game and version filled in ("Comentarios sobre Bichoku v…" / "Feedback on Bichoku v…"), plus a link to GitHub issues.
- **Support:** by build flag `store = play | itch`. Play: no donation links or wording. itch: links to the donation pages.
- **i18n:** shared strings (es/en), the device-language default (Spanish for `es*`, else English) and the language switch, saved in settings.
- **Sound:** effects player, music player, separate mute toggles, saved.
- **Theme:** a shared `Theme` base and palette slots (paper, line, accent, error) the game fills.
- **Mobile:** back button handling, pause on background, safe areas.

## Sound

Effects synthesized by a committed script (portfolio Sound section): place an animal, toggle a note, clash, hint, undo, win, and a firework pop. Music: one short calm loop, **on by default** (Juan, 2026-11-25; first my call was off).

- **Mute button** (Juan asked; the design is my call): a speaker next to "?" on the main screen silences music and effects together; tapping it again restores what the Music and Effects switches in Ajustes say. The switches stay for finer choices. The mute is saved.
- **Win fanfare** (Juan): six bursts of fireworks over the whole screen, the win sheet included, in the brighter animal inks, about 2.4 s; each burst pops. They never take a tap.

## Store and release

The portfolio's release process, in order: signed AAB with Play App Signing; web build and APK for itch.io; listings in es-419 and en-US (name, descriptions, icon, screenshots, feature graphic, all made in code in the engraving style); privacy policy on GitHub Pages; content rating, 13+, Data safety "no data collected"; closed test with the tester group for 14 days; production on Play; itch.io page (free, pay-what-you-want); make the repo public.

**Who does what (my call):** this game's build plan ends at a **release candidate**: the game complete, all checks passing, signed-ready Android export (Gradle build with the Android build template; the signing key is created by Juan and stays outside the repo), the web build, the APK, the store graphics and listing texts, and the privacy policy text. The steps that need Juan's accounts or approval (Play developer account, upload key, the tester group and the 14-day closed test, the GitHub org, pushing, GitHub Pages, the itch.io upload, the donation links) are a checklist handed to him; the portfolio's `release-game` skill will run them with him. The itch Support screen reads its links from one config file, so the itch build ships once the links exist.

**Blockers outside this repo before release:** the feedback email address (a dedicated one, never Juan's personal address), the GitHub org `churrinche-games` (for the source link and privacy policy page), the Play developer account.
